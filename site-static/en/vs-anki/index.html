MicroGrind vs Anki — MicroGrind advantages

MicroGrind is a study app that builds a Subject Map — topics, concepts, definitions and relations — from your course notes, handbook or slides, then runs short study sessions of 5 to 25 minutes. It works for any course or subject, from primary school to higher education.

This page shows what MicroGrind has that Anki does not, using verifiable product facts. (Anki remains strong at spaced-repetition flashcards — that is not MicroGrind’s ✓ row.)

What Anki does well

  • Flashcards with smart scheduling (spaced repetition), decks, AnkiWeb sync and add-ons — full control if you want to manage the deck.
  • Free desktop (Win/macOS/Linux); a huge community of shared decks.

Choose MicroGrind if…

  • You want the structure of the subject (Subject Map) before reviewing cards.
  • You prefer guided sessions of 5–25 minutes (mini-lesson + cards + questions) from the map, without building decks by hand.
  • You want to open on the web / PWA from the PDF or handbook you upload.

Common questions

Is MicroGrind a mind map / Anki replacement?

It is not a mind map. We do not present it as an Anki replacement. The differentiator is Subject Map + short sessions from the map. Anki remains ideal for spaced repetition with decks you control.

Can I use both?

Yes. Structure and sessions in MicroGrind; spaced card review in Anki if you want.

Does Anki lack flashcards that MicroGrind has?

Anki has flashcards (that is the product’s core). MicroGrind’s ✓ row is not “has flashcards”; it is map + session from the map.

Any course?

Yes — primary through higher education.

Which files?

PDF, Word, text, photos. Web + PWA («Coming soon on Android»).

Cost?

MicroGrind: N/A. Anki: free desktop and AnkiDroid; paid AnkiMobile (iOS) on the App Store — confirm there.
