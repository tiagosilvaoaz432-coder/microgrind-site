Developmental psychology

Developmental psychology exam questions

Ten multiple-choice questions on the main theories of development, with an explanation for each answer.

Mini quiz: Developmental psychology

Pick an option for each question, then open the answer to check.

  1. Question 1 of 10

    What is the correct order of Piaget’s stages of cognitive development?

    1. APreoperational, sensorimotor, formal operational, concrete operational
    2. BSensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational
    3. CSensorimotor, concrete operational, preoperational, formal operational
    4. DOral, anal, phallic, genital
    Show answer

    Correct answer: B) Sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational. Piaget’s stages run from birth to adolescence. Oral, anal, phallic and genital are Freud’s psychosexual stages.

  2. Question 2 of 10

    In which of Piaget’s stages is object permanence acquired?

    1. APreoperational
    2. BConcrete operational
    3. CSensorimotor
    4. DFormal operational
    Show answer

    Correct answer: C) Sensorimotor. During the first two years, the child comes to understand that objects continue to exist when they are out of sight.

  3. Question 3 of 10

    Egocentrism and symbolic thought are characteristic of which stage?

    1. ASensorimotor
    2. BConcrete operational
    3. CFormal operational
    4. DPreoperational
    Show answer

    Correct answer: D) Preoperational. From roughly 2 to 7 years of age, children use symbols and language but find it hard to take another person’s point of view.

  4. Question 4 of 10

    In which stage does conservation (for example, of a quantity of liquid) appear?

    1. AConcrete operational
    2. BSensorimotor
    3. CPreoperational
    4. DFormal operational
    Show answer

    Correct answer: A) Concrete operational. From roughly 7 to 11 years of age, children understand that a quantity stays the same despite changes in shape, thanks to the reversibility of thought.

  5. Question 5 of 10

    What is Vygotsky’s central concept for learning with help from others?

    1. AEquilibration
    2. BZone of proximal development
    3. CCritical period
    4. DOperant conditioning
    Show answer

    Correct answer: B) Zone of proximal development. It is the gap between what a child can do alone and what they can do with help from an adult or a more capable peer. Help that is adjusted to the child’s level is often called scaffolding.

  6. Question 6 of 10

    According to Erikson, what is the typical psychosocial crisis of adolescence?

    1. AIntimacy versus isolation
    2. BTrust versus mistrust
    3. CIdentity versus role confusion
    4. DGenerativity versus stagnation
    Show answer

    Correct answer: C) Identity versus role confusion. In adolescence, young people try to build a coherent identity. Intimacy versus isolation is the crisis of young adulthood.

  7. Question 7 of 10

    Which procedure, developed by Mary Ainsworth, assesses attachment in infants?

    1. AThe marshmallow test
    2. BThe three mountains task
    3. CThe Heinz dilemma
    4. DThe Strange Situation
    Show answer

    Correct answer: D) The Strange Situation. The Strange Situation observes how an infant reacts to separation from, and reunion with, the attachment figure. The three mountains task is Piaget’s and the Heinz dilemma is Kohlberg’s.

  8. Question 8 of 10

    In the Strange Situation, a securely attached infant typically:

    1. AIs distressed by the separation, and seeks and accepts comfort at reunion
    2. BIgnores the attachment figure at reunion
    3. CShows no reaction to either the separation or the reunion
    4. DShows anger and rejects comfort at reunion
    Show answer

    Correct answer: A) Is distressed by the separation, and seeks and accepts comfort at reunion. Ignoring the attachment figure at reunion is typical of insecure-avoidant attachment; seeking contact while resisting comfort is typical of insecure-ambivalent (resistant) attachment.

  9. Question 9 of 10

    Lawrence Kohlberg mainly studied:

    1. AMotor development
    2. BMoral development
    3. CLanguage development
    4. DPsychosexual development
    Show answer

    Correct answer: B) Moral development. Kohlberg proposed three levels of moral reasoning (preconventional, conventional and postconventional), based on responses to dilemmas such as the Heinz dilemma.

  10. Question 10 of 10

    According to Erikson, what is the first psychosocial crisis, in the first year of life?

    1. AAutonomy versus shame and doubt
    2. BInitiative versus guilt
    3. CTrust versus mistrust
    4. DIndustry versus inferiority
    Show answer

    Correct answer: C) Trust versus mistrust. Its outcome depends on how predictable and good the care is. The other options are the next crises of childhood, in that order.

How to study developmental psychology

Make a comparison table by theorist: Piaget, Vygotsky, Erikson, Kohlberg, Bowlby and Ainsworth. For each one, note the type of development studied, the stages or key concepts and the approximate ages.

Exam questions often test the order of the stages and matching a concrete example to the right concept. Practise with examples: a child who thinks there is more water in the tall glass is in which stage?

Also review the criticisms of each theory, which often come up in short-answer and essay questions.

Common questions

What is the difference between Piaget and Vygotsky?

Piaget puts more weight on the individual construction of knowledge, through stages. Vygotsky puts more weight on social interaction and culture, with the concept of the zone of proximal development.

How many stages are there in Erikson’s theory?

Eight, from birth to old age, each defined by a psychosocial crisis (for example, trust versus mistrust, or identity versus role confusion).
